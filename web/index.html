Show your robot the after.

Scan a table with your iPhone and say what you want. Ayna edits your own photo into the after, checks every object in it, lets robots rehearse it in a physics twin, and measures the result against that photo in centimetres.

01The problem

“Tidy up” hides a hundred decisions.

Which things move. Where each one ends up. What stays put. What no gripper can hold. A text plan never says where anything goes, so a robot guesses, and you find out after it has touched your things.

Robots touch things blindly.

You cannot see what a robot is about to do to your desk, or check the result when it is done. Today, for chores a home robot cannot do yet, owners can schedule a remote 1X Expert to guide NEO.

02How it works

One photo in. A checked plan out.

Five Meta models pass the work around a loop, and every step leaves something you can see: the words, the pictures, the verdicts, the plan. Everything below comes from one real run on the desk above.

  1. 1

    Muse Voice Transcribe

    Hears what you want, in your own words. Typing works too.

  2. 2

    Muse Spark

    Writes the edit for this exact photo: what moves, where to, and what must stay where it is.

  3. 3

    Muse Image

    Develops several versions of the after photo from your own picture, not a stock scene.

    • 4

      SAM 3.1

      Finds every object in every version. A version where something vanished, appeared or changed size is rejected with the reason, and Spark redraws it with a corrected instruction.

        Rejected versions never reach a robot.

      • 5

        Muse Spark

        Picks the version that follows the request best, orders the moves and gives each one to a robot that can do it, or refuses with the measurement.

      • 6

        SAM 3D Objects

        Every scanned object is a full 3D body in a MuJoCo twin of the table, so robots rehearse on the real shapes before anything real moves.

      Then the verifier renders the twin from the photo’s own camera and measures every object against the after photo, in centimetres.

      03The after photo

      The picture is the plan you approve.

      Before a robot touches anything, you see your own desk the way it will be, and say yes or no. Drag to compare.

      04Measured, not claimed

      Every run ends with a receipt.

      An object matches when it rests within 3 cm of where the after photo puts it, about half the smallest object on the desk (Meta’s Habitat Tidy House allows 15 cm at room scale). A refused or failed move always costs points.

      05Robots

      Two robots. Honest answers.

      Muse Spark orders the moves and gives each one to a robot that can reach and grip it. When none can, Ayna says so, with the measurement, instead of trying.

      Our 7-DoF arm
      Placed on the scanned table where it reaches the objects. Its gripper opens 11 cm.
      A mobile manipulator
      Plans where to park beside the surface, drives there along an A* path, raises its lift and picks from above, like the arm.

      Refusals from real runs

        06The Ayna iPhone app

        Scan once. Get a twin.

        Walk around the table with a LiDAR iPhone. The app records the table and its room, then asks for one hero shot: the before photo every after is edited from.

        • The LiDAR mesh is drawn over the camera: orange triangles still need photos, lime ones are covered.

        • Photos are taken automatically. Done turns lime at 3 walls, 8 photos and 85% photo detail.

        • For the hero shot, a level turns lime within 1.5°, and the still comes from the same ARKit session as the scan.

        07Who it is for

        The homes robots come to, and the teams who build them.

        People a robot comes home to

        They see the after photo and a rehearsal in their own twin, then approve or decline before anything moves. The picture is the consent screen.

        Robotics teams testing policies in real homes

        Each scan, after photo and verifier is an evaluation episode in a real home, with an automatic success check instead of hand-written task code.

        Goal-conditioned planners need a goal image: Meta’s V-JEPA 2-AC and FAIR’s RoboJEPA both plan toward one. In a stranger’s home nobody has it. Ayna makes it from the owner’s own photo and checks it first.
